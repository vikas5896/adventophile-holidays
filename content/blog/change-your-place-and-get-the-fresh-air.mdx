"Get some fresh air" sounds like something your grandmother says. It also happens to be one of the more consistently supported pieces of low-effort wellness advice out there — time in green or blue space (forests, mountains, open water) is linked to measurably lower stress markers, and the effect shows up faster than most people expect.

## You don't need two weeks

The instinct is to save "real" travel for one big trip a year and treat everything else as maintenance. We'd push back on that. A three-day trip — our Blue City Jodhpur & Osian Desert Safari, or a weekend built around Landmannalaugar's hot springs — delivers a version of the same reset a longer trip does, just compressed. The change of environment is doing most of the work, not the length of the stay.

## Water changes the effect

There's a reason "blue space" gets called out separately from green space in a lot of the wellness literature — being near or on open water seems to have an outsized calming effect compared to land-based nature alone. It's part of why paddling trips like the Discovery Islands kayaking route get some of our strongest repeat-traveler feedback: the water itself seems to be doing something the scenery alone wouldn't.

> You don't have to summit anything for a trip to count as a reset. Sometimes the fresh air is the whole point.

## Building it into a normal year

Our suggestion to travelers who feel too busy for a big annual trip: swap it for two or three shorter ones spread through the year instead of saving everything for a single two-week block. The cumulative reset tends to beat the single dose.
