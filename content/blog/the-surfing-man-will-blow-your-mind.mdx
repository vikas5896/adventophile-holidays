Most sea kayak guides come up through paddling clubs. Our lead guide on the Discovery Islands trip, came up through surfing — a decade on a board off Vancouver Island before he ever sat in a kayak. It shows in a way that's hard to describe until you've paddled behind him through a tide race.

## Reading water like a surfer, not a paddler

Surfers spend years learning to read a wave before it breaks: where the energy is building, where it's already spent. That instinct transfers directly to reading tidal currents, which is the single hardest skill in sea kayaking around the Discovery Islands' notoriously strong channels. Where a purely paddling-trained guide might follow a chart, he reads the surface itself — the smoothness, the boils, the subtle colour change where two currents meet.

> "A wave and a tide race are the same conversation, just at different speeds. The water is always telling you where it wants to go. You just have to stop paddling long enough to listen."

## Why that matters for a multi-day trip

On a four-day guided trip, that reading ability is the difference between paddling with a current and fighting one for three hours. It's also part of why the wildlife encounters on this route are so consistent — knowing where currents concentrate baitfish means knowing where orcas and seals are most likely to be feeding.

## Try it yourself

No surfing background required — the Discovery Islands Kayaking Tour is built for first-time paddlers, with all technique taught on day one before the group heads into open water.
