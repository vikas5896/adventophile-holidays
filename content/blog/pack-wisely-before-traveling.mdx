Most packing advice is really luggage advice — buy this bag, use these cubes. That's useful, but it skips the harder question: what actually goes in it? After years of leading trips from desert camps to high-altitude teahouses, here's the system we tell every traveler before they book.

## Pack in layers, not outfits

Instead of thinking "what will I wear on day 4," think in three layers: a base layer that manages moisture, a mid layer for warmth, and a shell for wind and rain. Three of each layer, mixed and matched, covers a wider temperature range than eight separate outfits — and weighs less.

## One pair of shoes does more than you think

A well broken-in pair of trail shoes handles a city walking tour and a moderate day hike equally well. Save the second pair of shoes for something your trip specifically requires — reef shoes for a kayaking trip, insulated boots for Kilimanjaro — not "just in case."

> The travelers who pack lightest are almost always the ones who've done the trip before. Pack like it's your second time, not your first.

## Check the trip's included gear list first

Several of our multi-day treks and camping trips — the Everest Base Camp trek and the Discovery Islands kayaking tour among them — already include tents, sleeping mats, and cooking gear. Packing your own is not just unnecessary weight, it can mean carrying redundant equipment for a week. Always check what's listed under "Included" on the tour page before you start shopping.

## The one thing worth overpacking

Batteries and power banks. Cold weather drains phone batteries fast, and remote lodges don't always have reliable outlets. A single 10,000mAh power bank has saved more trips than any other item on this list.
